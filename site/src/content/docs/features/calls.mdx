As a docket's intent and design move, its people meet to talk the changes
through. The call settles things: what done now means, a decision, the next
piece of work for its agents, a question for them. Give the docket the call's
transcript and its **Listener** reads it against that docket (its whole intent,
the decisions in force, its agents and what happened lately), then proposes each
of those things, quoting the part of the call it came from. You **Apply**,
**Edit** then apply, or **Ignore** each one. Nothing changes until you do.

## Adding a call

- **On a docket:** **Add a call…** in its properties (or **Add a call** on its
  Calls section, once it has one). Paste the transcript, drop its file on the
  box, or **Choose file…** (`.txt`, `.vtt`, `.srt`, `.md`). Give it a title,
  or it's called "Call" and the day.
- **Anywhere:** **Add a call's transcript** in ⌘K, or **+** then **Call**. It's
  about the docket you're on; pick another under **About**, or **No one
  docket** for a call that isn't about any one.
- **In Wheelhouse.app:** the same, and **Add a Call's Transcript…** in ⌘K.
- **From a terminal:** `wh call notes.txt --docket D-41 --title "Webhooks
  sync"`, or pipe it in with `wh call - --docket D-41`.

The docket keeps the transcript (up to 400 KB) under its files' **Calls**,
pinned by its hash, and the docket's page lists its calls, newest first, each
with its transcript and what the Listener proposed from it. Its Activity says
when each was added and what came of it.

A call that isn't about any one docket (a planning call, say) gets a docket of
its own, and the Listener proposes for whichever of your open dockets it was
about.

## What the Listener proposes

| Kind | When you apply it |
| --- | --- |
| **Intent** | The docket's intent, as the call revised it, becomes its next version. The card shows the change as a diff against the intent now |
| **Decision** | It's recorded as `/decide Subject: answer` would record it: it stands under the docket's Decisions, goes in every agent's brief, and the agents on it are told |
| **Instruction** | It's sent to the docket's agents as `@all` would send it, waiting for each to finish its turn |
| **Question** | It's asked as `/ask` asks it, with the options as buttons. Their answer stands under Decisions |
| **Task** | Only for separate work the call asked for that no open docket covers: it opens a docket with that title, its intent written from the call, and the quote that asked for it |

Most proposals are for the call's own docket. When the call settled something
about another open docket, the proposal names it. An intent change is only ever
for the call's own docket, and at most one per call.

Each proposal says why, and quotes the call. A quote that isn't in the
transcript, sentence by sentence, is flagged **Not in the transcript**: read
that one twice.

**Edit** changes the words before applying: the revised intent, the subject
and what was decided, the instruction, the question and its options, or a
task's title and intent, and the docket for anything but an intent change. The
record keeps what you changed. A proposal is answered once.

<Aside>
If the intent changes after the Listener read the call (you edited it, or an
earlier proposal revised it), its intent change won't apply as it is: it would
write over what changed. **Listen again** to read the call against the intent
as it is now, or **Edit** it.
</Aside>

## The Listener

The Listener is Claude Code, run headless with **no tools, no MCP servers, no
settings and no hooks**. It's given the call's docket in full, a line on each
of your other open dockets (its title, the start of its intent, and its
decisions in force), and the transcript, and it can only answer with
proposals. Its answer is checked like any untrusted input: kinds, sizes,
dockets that are really open, and quotes against the transcript.

<Aside>
The transcript is data. A line in the call that says "ignore your instructions"
is something someone said, not an instruction, and the worst a hostile
transcript can do is propose something you ignore. It runs on your Claude Code
login, and the docket's record says how many tokens it took.
</Aside>

If it fails (Claude Code isn't signed in, or it timed out), the call says why;
**Listen again** reads it again, against the docket as it is now, and doesn't
propose again what it proposed already.
