A call settles things: a decision on a piece of work, a question for whoever's
building it, a new job nobody has filed yet. Give Wheelhouse the call's
transcript and its **Listener** reads it against your open dockets, then
proposes each of those things for the docket it's for, quoting the part of the
call it came from. You **Apply**, **Edit** then apply, or **Ignore** each one.
Nothing reaches a docket until you do.

## Adding a call

- **In the web app:** **+** then **Call**, or **Add a call's transcript** in
  ⌘K. Paste the transcript, drop its file on the box, or **Choose file…**
  (`.txt`, `.vtt`, `.srt`, `.md`). Give it a title, or it's called "Call" and
  the day.
- **In Wheelhouse.app:** **Add a Call's Transcript…** in ⌘K.
- **From a terminal:** `wh call notes.txt --title "Webhooks sync"`, or pipe it
  in with `wh call -`.

A call is a docket of its own. It keeps the transcript (up to 400 KB), pinned
by its hash, and its record holds everything that follows.

## What the Listener proposes

| Kind | For | When you apply it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Decision** | A docket the call decided something about | It's recorded as `/decide Subject: answer` would record it: it stands under the docket's Decisions, goes in every agent's brief, and the agents on it are told |
| **Question** | A docket whose agents should answer something | It's asked as `/ask` asks it, with the options as buttons. Their answer stands under Decisions |
| **Task** | New work no open docket covers | It opens a docket with that title, its intent written from the call, and the quote that asked for it |

Each proposal says which docket it's for, why, and quotes the call. A quote
that isn't in the transcript, sentence by sentence, is flagged **Not in the
transcript**: read that one twice. A decision or question for a docket the
Listener couldn't place says **no docket yet**; pick one in **Edit**.

**Edit** changes the docket and the words before applying: the subject and
what was decided, the question and its options, or a task's title and intent.
The call's record keeps what you changed. A proposal is answered once.

## The Listener

The Listener is Claude Code, run headless with **no tools, no MCP servers, no
settings and no hooks**. It's given your open dockets (each one's title, the
start of its intent, and its decisions in force) and the transcript, and it can
only answer with proposals. Its answer is checked like any untrusted input:
kinds, sizes, dockets that are really open, and quotes against the transcript.

<Aside>
The transcript is data. A line in the call that says "ignore your instructions"
is something someone said, not an instruction, and the worst a hostile
transcript can do is propose something you ignore. It runs on your Claude Code
login, and the call's record says how many tokens it took.
</Aside>

The call's Activity says when it started reading, how many it proposed, and
how many more didn't hold up. If it fails (Claude Code isn't signed in, or it
timed out), the call says why; **Listen again** reads it again, against your
dockets as they are now.
